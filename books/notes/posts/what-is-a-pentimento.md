Title: What is a pentimento?
Date: 2026-10-30
Book: pentimento
Summary: The word means repentance. In a painting it’s the trace of something the artist changed their mind about, coming back through the paint years later. It’s also where the Provenance Series begins.

Look closely at an old oil painting and you’ll sometimes find a ghost. A second outline of an arm. A hat that seems to have been in two places at once. A faint shape in the sky that doesn’t belong to anything.

That ghost is a pentimento. The word comes from the Italian *pentirsi*, to repent or change your mind, and it means a change the painter made while working: something painted, then painted over.

## Why the past shows through

Oil paint isn’t as solid as it looks. Over decades and centuries, the upper layers can become more transparent, and what was hidden underneath begins to come back. The painter who covered it never meant you to see it. Time decided otherwise.

Velázquez’s portrait of King Philip IV of Spain, in the Frick Collection in New York, is full of second thoughts. When conservators studied it, they found he had first painted the king’s hat lower, given the coat a wider, flared outline and had the sword a little higher. Some of those changes can be seen with the naked eye.

Conservators don’t have to wait for time, either. X-rays pick up heavy pigments in the buried layers, and infrared can show the drawing beneath the paint. A painting can be read in layers, like a letter that has been written over.

## A change of mind, or a cover-up

A pentimento is the painter’s own change. Sometimes the change was made by somebody else.

For centuries, Vermeer’s *Girl Reading a Letter at an Open Window*, in Dresden, showed a young woman reading by a window in front of a bare wall. An X-ray in 1979 found a painting of Cupid hidden on that wall, and for years it was thought Vermeer had painted him out himself. Then a restoration that began in 2017 showed the overpaint had gone on long after Vermeer’s death, by another hand. The museum removed it, and since 2021 Cupid has been back on the wall.

That’s the difference. One is a painter thinking. The other is someone deciding what you’re allowed to see.

## Why the books are called what they’re called

Dr Alec Muir, at the centre of [*Pentimento*](pentimento/), authenticates paintings. Nobody in Scotland is better at seeing what lies beneath a surface, and nobody has worked harder to keep people from seeing what lies beneath his. The book takes the word at its meaning: the past doesn’t stay buried. It shows through.

[*Underpainting*](underpainting/), the second book, takes the other word. An underpainting is the first layer of a picture, often plain grey, laid down to fix where the light and shadow will fall and meant to disappear under everything that follows. In the book, a woman clearing her late father’s house takes down the portrait that has hung on his stairs all her life, the grey lady, and starts asking who she was.

Beneath the grey lady is another woman. Someone has spent a very long time making sure nobody saw her.
